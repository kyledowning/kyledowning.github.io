Lightning Network research

Graduate research, 2025 to present
[Advised by Prof. X], WWU Computer Science

Lightning Network channels drain in one direction as payments route through them, and a one-sided channel stops forwarding. The standard remedy is cyclic rebalancing, which refills a channel with a circular self-payment and pays routing fees to do it. This research asks whether a node can instead refill its channels by repricing its own outbound fees and letting organic traffic do the work.

  • Built a discrete-event Lightning Network simulator in Python with no dependencies outside the standard library, modeling directional channel policies, HTLC atomicity, balance conservation, and route learning over synthetic Watts-Strogatz topologies.
  • Designed FLEX, a fee controller that pivots its fee curve on a user-specified target balance and discounts a channel only if it passes an edge betweenness centrality condition, leaving the rest at their original prices.
  • Evaluated FLEX against no intervention, cyclic rebalancing, and an unconditional fee controller across three capacity classes and 10 seeds each. FLEX restored drained channels fastest in every class, at 650, 1,690, and 4,650 transactions.
  • FLEX earned more in all 30 paired runs, finishing the median class at 236,839 sat net against 215,796 for the unconditional controller and 79,809 for cyclic rebalancing. Since both fee schemes apply identical fee magnitudes, the margin comes entirely from the discounts the condition withheld, at no cost to recovery speed.

Python, discrete-event simulation, graph algorithms, matplotlib, LaTeX
